# REEP — the launch speech

*For the person on stage. About ten minutes spoken. Two movements: the first to
the students, the second to the faculty. The refrain is "Ready is not a
feeling. Ready is a record." Every claim in it is something the portal does;
nothing here promises a placement, only readiness. Pause where the lines break.*

---

## Part one — to the students

Jai Shri Gurudev.

Let me start with a story. A student, let's call her Priya, opens a form on
her phone. Name. USN. Phone number. Her CV. Her photograph. She presses
Submit. She has just applied to join this programme, and she has no idea yet
that she is about to be seen more clearly than any student before her.

Because the moment the placement office approves her, the portal builds her a
home. Her college, her department, her course, her batch — already filled in.
A link arrives in her mail. She types her address, she types a code, she
chooses a password. And when she signs in for the first time, her name is on
the screen, her batch is on the screen, and three words are waiting for her:
**Reboot. Excel. Elevate.**

That is where every one of you is right now. At the start of a record.

Let me say something about how we got here, because you should know what has
changed.

Your seniors prepared for placements with a notice board, a WhatsApp group and
a rumour. *They want eight CGPA. Somebody's cousin said the HR round is easy.*
A student's readiness lived in a dozen spreadsheets nobody could see, and the
first honest feedback most of them ever received came from the interviewer who
rejected them.

Technology has moved. The interviewer on the other side of the table now uses
data to decide in twenty minutes. The workplace you are walking into runs on
dashboards, on evidence, on people who can say exactly what they know and
prove it. So the preparation had to move too. That is what REEP is: the
placement office, your mentors and your own effort, on **one screen, every
morning**, telling you the truth.

And here is the truth I want you to carry out of this hall:
**Ready is not a feeling. Ready is a record.**

Look at what that record holds.

Your Home shows the three stages and every module in them: done, in progress,
not started. No guessing where you stand. Beneath them, four lines. Your
strengths. Your weaknesses. Your opportunities. Your challenges. Written by
your mentor and by the placement office, each one signed with a name and a
date. In the old world, that was said about you in a staff room. In this one,
it is said **to** you, on your own screen, and you can mark that you have read
it.

**Skilling.** Forty-eight badges. Managerial. Sectoral. Technical. Thinking.
Readiness. Not one of them can be claimed. Every one must be earned: you do
the course, you upload the certificate, a mentor checks it and lights the
badge with their own name behind it. That blue tick is the new methodology in
one symbol. Learning is not what you sat through. Learning is what you can
show.

**Jobs.** Every opening the office posts appears with a number beside it: your
match. The criteria are the same for everyone, and the portal does the
arithmetic in the open. Eligible, it says so. Not yet, it tells you which line
you miss, so you fix the line instead of fearing the list.

**The Time Sheet.** Twenty-four hours, six slots, five heads, and the day must
add up before you can submit it. Nobody is policing your sleep. This is the
oldest discipline of every serious professional: look at a week and say
honestly, *that is where my hours went.* Half an hour unaccounted for is the
most useful thing this portal will ever show you.

**Your mentor, one button away.** Ask for a meeting from your log and it lands
on their desk. Every note they write afterwards, you read. Nothing is said
about you that is not also said to you.

And then the piece your seniors never had. The **mock interview**. A real
interviewer's voice. A real campus-round arc: opening, probing, deep dive,
wrap-up. A scorecard at the end that tells you where you were strong and
where you stumbled. You can face it at eleven at night in your hostel room.
You can face it eight times in a day. Nobody in this hall will walk into their
first real interview having never heard *tell me about yourself* spoken to
their face.

That is modern learning. Not a lecture you attend. A loop you run: attempt,
feedback, attempt again, with the feedback arriving in hours, not at the end
of the semester. And measured against **you**. The leaderboard that matters
most on this portal is not the one for points. It is the one for growth: how
far you have come from your own starting line. In here, the student who moved
the most is the student we celebrate.

Now let me speak about trust, because none of this works without it.

You are about to hand this college a truthful picture of yourself. So hear
what the college has built to deserve it. Your record does not leave our
machines without permission; that is a rule written into the software, not a
promise on a poster. Nothing is recorded in a mock interview until you have
read, in plain words, what will be kept and for how long, and agreed. Only the
people who are meant to see you can see you: your mentor, the placement
office. Nobody self-appoints. Every decision made about you is written on a
trail with a name, a reason and a time. And you sign in through one door,
with one live session, so that if you are ever signed out, the screen tells
you why.

That is digital trust. Not a feeling either. A set of things the system
refuses to do.

So use the record. Fill the time sheet honestly, because it is your mirror.
Claim the badges you have earned, and earn the ones you have not. Face the
interviewer before the interviewer faces you. Read what your mentors write and
answer it with work.

The portal does not make you ready. **You** make you ready. What it does is
take away every excuse: *I didn't know where I stood. I didn't know what they
wanted. I didn't know whom to ask.* From this morning, you know.

Ready is not a feeling. Ready is a record. Go and build yours.

## Part two — to the faculty

Now I turn to my colleagues, because the second half of this portal is yours,
and it is the half that decides whether the first half means anything.

When you sign in, you see your mentees. Not the whole college. **Your** group,
the students the office placed with you. Their attendance and their marks.
Their uploads and their badge claims. Their meeting requests. Their interview
reports. That is a complete picture of a young person, and it is being handed
to you for one reason: so that you can guide them better than anyone guided
us.

Use it for that.

When attendance slips, you will see it in the week it happens, not in the
month the results come out. When a certificate lands in your verification
queue, open it within two working days and either light the badge or say, in
one clear sentence, what is missing. When a student's four SWOC lines are
blank, fill them; a student with no strengths written down has been given
nothing to build on. When the interview scorecard names the same weakness
three times, your next meeting has already been planned for you. The portal
shows you the moment to act. Acting is still ours.

And it changes how we teach. For years we assessed at the end and hoped the
lesson had landed. This portal lets us assess as we go: a short note after
every meeting, a verdict on every certificate, a checkpoint on every
capability from the first week to the last, so that growth is something we
can point to, not something we assume. The student reads every one of those
notes. That is not a burden. That is the method. Feedback that is seen is
feedback that works.

Now the part I will say slowly, because it matters more than everything
before it.

**There are things this portal is not for.**

It is not for ranking a student by their circumstances. A dip in attendance, a
leave, a low English baseline: these are things to help with, never things to
hold against anyone. Assess the work. Never the situation.

It is not a scoreboard for judgement. A score that was never measured shows on
the screen as a dash, not a zero, and that was built in on purpose. Do not
count what was never measured. A readiness number is where a conversation
begins, not where a verdict ends.

It is not for sharing. What you see is scoped to your group, and it stays
there. No screenshots into a group chat. No forwarding a student's marks, USN
or interview transcript to anyone the portal itself would not show it to. And
no pasting a student's record into some outside tool to save yourself ten
minutes. The college built this so that student data does not leave our
machines unbidden. That rule binds us as much as it binds the software, and
every one of you holding a console screen holds it because the office granted
it, with a reason, on a trail, for a period. That is what makes the students'
trust rational.

It is not for writing what you would not say to the student's face. Every
meeting note you save, they read. Every SWOC line carries your name. Write as
a mentor, in words a nineteen-year-old can act on tomorrow.

It is not for favours. A badge is verified because the certificate is genuine,
not because you like the student. A rejection carries a reason, in writing,
that the student receives by mail and can fix. Same criteria, same evidence,
same timeline, for every student in your group. That is what fair assessment
means, and the audit trail records every decision so that fairness can be
shown, not merely claimed.

And it is not for surveillance. A recording exists only with the student's
consent, shown to them in plain words, and they can withdraw it. The Time
Sheet is the student's mirror, not our stick.

Colleagues, technology has given us something no generation of teachers had
before: the whole picture of a young person's year, in time to change it. The
measure of this portal will not be how much it shows us. It will be how
carefully, how fairly, how honestly we use what it shows.

Guide them with it. Assess them fairly on it. Protect what it holds.

Do those three things, and the students in this hall will leave with more than
an offer letter. They will leave with a record they built, verified by people
they trust, and they will know, from their own screen, exactly how they
earned it.

Ready is not a feeling. Ready is a record. Let us help them write it.

Thank you. Jai Shri Gurudev.
