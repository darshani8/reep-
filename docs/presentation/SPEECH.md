# REEP — a speech for the launch

*For the person on stage. About seven minutes spoken. Two movements: the first
to the students, the second to the faculty. Every claim in it is something the
portal does; nothing here promises a placement, only readiness.*

---

## Part one — to the students

Jai Shri Gurudev.

Look around this hall. Every one of you walked in with the same question, and
nobody says it out loud: *am I ready?* Ready for the interview panel. Ready for
the company that will decide in twenty minutes what your first year of working
life looks like. Ready to stand next to students from a hundred other colleges
and not flinch.

Today I want to give you an honest answer to that question. Not a feeling. Not
a rumour from a senior. An answer you can see, every morning, on one screen.

That screen is REEP.

You sign in once. The moment you do, the portal already knows who you are, which
batch you belong to, which course, which department, because the office put you
there. You never type it twice. What you see first is your Home: three stages,
**Reboot, Excel, Elevate**, and every module in them marked as done, in
progress, or not started. No guessing where you stand. It is written down, and
it is yours.

Under it, four lines. Your strengths. Your weaknesses. Your opportunities. Your
challenges. Written by your mentor and by the placement office, with a name and
a date on each one. When someone who has watched a thousand students says
*this is where you are strong*, you deserve to read it in their words. And when
they say *this is where you must work*, you deserve that too, before an
interviewer says it for you.

Then the part that changes lives. **Skilling.** Forty-eight badges: managerial,
sectoral, technical, thinking, readiness. Every one of them earned the same way:
you do the course, you upload the certificate, and a mentor verifies it. Not
claimed. Verified. When that badge lights up on your board with a blue tick,
it is not decoration. It is a fact about you that a recruiter can trust,
because a teacher put their name behind it.

**Jobs.** Every opening the placement office posts appears on your screen with
one number beside it: your match. Not a rumour that "they want eight CGPA".
The criteria are the same for everyone and the portal does the arithmetic. If
you are eligible, it says so. If you are not, it tells you which line you miss,
so you can go and fix that line instead of wondering.

**The Time Sheet.** Twenty-four hours, six slots, five heads. The day must add
up before you can submit it. Not because anyone wants to police your sleep,
but because the most successful people in this room will be the ones who can
look at a week and say, honestly, *that is where my hours went.* Half an hour
unaccounted for is the most useful thing the portal will ever show you.

**Your mentor, one button away.** Ask for a meeting from your log; it lands on
their desk. Every note they write about the meeting, you read. Nothing is said
about you that is not also said to you.

And when you are ready to be tested: the **mock interview**. A real interviewer
voice, a real campus-round arc, opening, probing, deep dive, wrap-up, and a
scorecard at the end. You can do it at eleven at night in your hostel room. You
can do it eight times in a day. Nobody in this hall will ever again walk into
their first real interview having never heard the question *tell me about
yourself* spoken to their face.

Here is what all of this adds up to. The portal does not make you ready.
**You** make you ready. What the portal does is take away every excuse:
*I didn't know where I stood. I didn't know what they wanted. I didn't know
whom to ask.* From today, you know. Every morning. On one screen.

Use it like that, and the question you walked in with has an answer.

---

## Part two — to the faculty

Now I turn to my colleagues, because the second half of this portal is
yours, and it is the half that decides whether the first half means anything.

When you sign in, you see your mentees. Not the whole college. **Your** group,
the students the office placed with you. Their attendance, their marks, their
uploads, their badge claims, their meeting requests, their interview reports.
That is a complete picture of a young person, and the college is handing it to
you for one reason: so that you can guide them better than anyone has guided
them before.

Use it for that. When a student's attendance slips, you will see it before the
semester ends, not after. When a certificate arrives in your verification
queue, look at it within two working days, and light the badge or say clearly
what is missing. When the four SWOC lines are blank on a student's Home, fill
them; a student with no strengths written down has been given nothing to build
on. When the interview scorecard says the same weakness three times, that is
your next meeting, already planned for you. The portal shows you the moment to
act. Acting is still ours.

And now the part I will say slowly, because it matters more than everything
before it.

**There are things this portal is not for.**

It is not for ranking a student by their circumstances. A dip in attendance, a
leave, a weak English baseline score: these are things to help with, not
things to hold against anyone. Assess the work. Never the person's situation.

It is not a scoreboard for judgement. A missing score shows on the screen as a
dash, never as a zero, and that was built in on purpose. Do not count what was
never measured. A readiness number is a place to begin a conversation, not a
verdict to end one.

It is not for sharing. What you see is scoped to your group, and it stays
there. No screenshots to a group chat. No forwarding a student's marks, USN or
interview transcript to anyone the portal itself would not show it to. And no
pasting a student's record into some outside tool to save yourself ten
minutes; the college built this so that student data does not leave our
machines without permission, and that rule applies to us as much as to the
software.

It is not for writing what you would not say to the student's face. Every
meeting note you save, they read. Every SWOC line carries your name. Write as
a mentor, in words the student can act on.

It is not for favours. A badge is verified because the certificate is genuine,
not because you like the student. A rejection carries a reason, in writing,
that the student receives by mail and can fix. Same criteria, same evidence,
same timeline, for every student in your group. That is what fair assessment
means, and the audit trail records every decision so that it can be shown to
be fair.

And it is not for surveillance. Recordings happen only with the student's
consent, and the consent is shown to them in plain words. The Time Sheet is the
student's mirror, not our stick.

Colleagues, we are being trusted with the whole picture of a young person's
year. The measure of this portal will not be how much it shows us. It will be
how carefully we use what it shows.

Guide them with it. Assess them fairly on it. Protect what it holds. Do those
three things, and every student in this hall will leave with more than a job
offer. They will leave knowing, from their own screen, exactly how they earned
it.

Thank you. Jai Shri Gurudev.
